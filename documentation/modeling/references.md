# References: Collectives Explainer Series

**Author:** Yue Lu  
**Date:** April 2026  

Self-contained bibliography for the five chapters in this folder, `01_collective_algorithms.md` to `05_contention_and_congestion.md`. The equations and empirical values in the series are traced to the sources below; the verification notes at the end say which values are vendor-published, which are community-reported, and which are estimates.

## Primary algorithm papers

**[ALPHA-BETA]** Hockney, R. (1994). *The Communication Challenge for MPP: Intel Paragon and Meiko CS-2.* Parallel Computing 20(3). → Canonical α-β latency model $t = \alpha + M/\mathrm{BW}$. Used throughout `01_collective_algorithms.md` §1.

**[PY09]** Patarasuk, P., & Yuan, X. (2009). *Bandwidth Optimal All-Reduce Algorithms for Clusters of Workstations.* JPDC 69(2):117–124. → Ring AR achieves $2(N-1)/N \cdot M/\mathrm{BW}$ on any tree-connected fabric. Backs up `01_collective_algorithms.md` §5.1 and `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpdc.2008.09.002>

**[TRG05]** Thakur, R., Rabenseifner, R., & Gropp, W. (2005). *Optimization of Collective Communication Operations in MPICH.* IJHPCA 19(1):49–66. → Under an α-β model with single-ported nodes (§3), gives the costs of recursive-doubling all-gather (§4.1.1) and recursive-halving reduce-scatter (§4.4), each $\lg p \cdot \alpha + \frac{p-1}{p} n\beta$ for power-of-2 $p$; of simple recursive-doubling all-reduce and of Rabenseifner's reduce-scatter + all-gather all-reduce (§4.5; restated in §5.1 with the non-power-of-2 fold) — the latter $2\lceil \log_2 N \rceil \alpha + 2(N-1)/N \cdot M/\mathrm{BW}$ for power-of-2 N, plus a local-reduction term that the α-β form drops; and of Bruck and pairwise-exchange all-to-all (§4.3). Also records the experimentally determined message-size and process-count cutoffs at which MPICH 1.2.6 / MPICH2 1.0 switched algorithms for all-gather, broadcast, all-to-all, reduce-scatter, reduce and all-reduce (§4.1.3, §4.2–§4.5). Backs up `01_collective_algorithms.md` §6, Appendix B.2, Appendix B.3 and Appendix B.4; background for `02_topology_mapping.md` Appendix A (the paper assumes a flat network and does not treat tori). <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094342005051521>, author preprint: <https://web.cels.anl.gov/~thakur/papers/ijhpca-coll.pdf>

**[MPI-SELECT]** Open MPI and MPICH source code (read at tags v4.0.7, v5.0.6 and v4.2.3). Open MPI: `ompi/mca/coll/tuned/coll_tuned_decision_fixed.c` (the fixed decision rules for Broadcast and Reduce). MPICH: `src/mpi/coll/coll_algorithms.txt` (algorithm menus) and `maint/tuning/coll/mpir/generic.json` (default selection); also the cost header of `src/mpi/coll/allreduce/allreduce_intra_reduce_scatter_allgather.c` ($(2\lfloor \lg p \rfloor + 2)\alpha$ for a non-power-of-two process count). → Which broadcast and reduce algorithm each library selects by message size and communicator size. Backs up the library statements in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §3, §3.3, §4.1, §4.3 and Appendix B.3. <https://github.com/open-mpi/ompi>, <https://github.com/pmodels/mpich>

**[CHPV07]** Chan, E., Heimlich, M., Purkayastha, A., & van de Geijn, R. (2007). *Collective Communication: Theory, Practice, and Experience.* Concurrency and Computation: Practice and Experience, 19(13):1749–1783. → Dim-decomposed all-reduce framework and telescoping derivation of multi-dim ring costs. Backs up the torus dim-decomp cost and BW telescoping in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.1.

**[BHKUW97]** Bruck, J., Ho, C.-T., Kipnis, S., Upfal, E., & Weathersby, D. (1997). *Efficient Algorithms for All-to-All Communications in Multiport Message-Passing Systems.* IEEE TPDS 8(11):1143–1156. → Log-round all-to-all via circular shifts and bit-wise exchange; $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ steps, each moving up to half of a rank's payload (at most $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor$ of its $N$ blocks), so $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil \cdot M/2$ in total against the minimum $(N-1)/N \cdot M$. Backs up the "log A2A" variant in `01_collective_algorithms.md` Appendix B.5. <https://doi.org/10.1109/71.642949>

**[SST09]** Sanders, P., Speck, J., & Träff, J.L. (2009). *Two-Tree Algorithms for Full Bandwidth Broadcast, Reduction and Scan.* Parallel Computing 35(12):581–594. → Double binary tree (DBT) construction: two complementary trees that each rank is interior in exactly one of, so that both directions of every rank's port carry traffic during broadcast / reduce / AR. Backs up the DBT derivation in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §5.2, the DBT shipping rationale in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §5.3, and the single-port two-tree BC / Reduce form in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §3.2 and §4.2. The paper's own model is single-ported with one send and one receive per step, in which the two-tree broadcast and reduce cost $2\alpha\log_2 N + M/\mathrm{BW}$ to leading order; this series quotes $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil \cdot \alpha$ under its one-α-per-tree-level convention (`01_collective_algorithms.md` §3.2, App. C.4). The paper is not openly available and was not read directly (November 2026): the step count $(2h + 2k)$ quoted in §3.2 is the form in the Wikipedia article "Two-tree broadcast", and the first author's lecture slides [SANDERS-PA] agree on the leading terms; every statement of this series about the paper's model rests on those two sources. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parco.2009.09.001>

**[SANDERS-PA]** Sanders, P. (2022). *Parallel Algorithms* (lecture slides, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, winter term 2021/22; broadcast chapter). → Restates the two-tree broadcast of [SST09] in the fully connected, single-ported, full-duplex model: each step costs α plus the segment's transfer time, the first segment needs two steps per tree level ($2d + k - 1$ steps for $k$ segments, $d \approx \log_2 N$), and the total is about $M/\mathrm{BW} + 2\alpha\log_2 N$ plus a square-root term; reduction is the same schedule reversed. Backs up the step-count convention note in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §3.2. <https://ae.iti.kit.edu/documents/teaching/parallele-algorithmen/ws21/vorlesung.pdf>

**[TRAFF22]** Träff, J.L. (2022). *(Poly)Logarithmic Time Construction of Round-optimal n-Block Broadcast Schedules for Broadcast and irregular Allgather in MPI.* arXiv:2205.10072 (results published at SPAA 2022 and CLUSTER 2022). → In a 1-ported network with fully bidirectional communication, broadcasting $n$ blocks to $p$ processors takes $n - 1 + \lceil \log_2 p \rceil$ rounds, and schedules reaching that bound exist for every $p$. Backs up the remark in `01_collective_algorithms.md` §3.2 that $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil \cdot \alpha + M/\mathrm{BW}$ is attainable even under strict one-message-per-step accounting. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10072>

**[LEIS85]** Leiserson, C.E. (1985). *Fat-Trees: Universal Networks for Hardware-Efficient Supercomputing.* IEEE Transactions on Computers C-34(10):892–901. → Foundational fat-tree network construction: universal routing with bandwidth that grows fatter toward the root; the basis for Clos topology analysis in modern AI fabrics. Backs up the fat-tree / Clos topology treatment in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1 and the Leiserson-vs-Al-Fares comparison in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` Appendix B. <https://doi.org/10.1109/TC.1985.6312192>

**[AL-FARES08]** Al-Fares, M., Loukissas, A., & Vahdat, A. (2008). *A Scalable, Commodity Data Center Network Architecture.* ACM SIGCOMM 2008, pp. 63–74. → The k-ary fat-tree construction from commodity k-port switches: each switch splits its k ports k/2 up / k/2 down, yielding a rearrangeably non-blocking Clos with $s = 1$ at every tier. The equivalence "k-ary fat-tree = Clos with $s = 1$" that modern datacenter fabrics rely on is proved constructively in §3. Backs up the Clos-vs-fat-tree distinction in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1 and the k-ary fat-tree derivation in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` Appendix B. <https://doi.org/10.1145/1402958.1402967>

**[THAKUR-HIER]** Thakur, R., & Gropp, W. (2003). *Improving the Performance of Collective Operations in MPICH.* EuroPVM/MPI 2003, LNCS 2840:257–267. → Message-size-tuned algorithm selection for MPICH collectives (allgather, broadcast, reduce-scatter, reduce) — the conference precursor of [TRG05] — benchmarked on a Myrinet Linux cluster and an IBM SP. Background for the MPI algorithm menu in `01_collective_algorithms.md` Appendix B. It is not a hierarchical-scheduling paper; for the tier-aware pattern of `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §2.1 see [MAGPIE], [ZGGT09] and [HAN]. <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-39924-7_38>

**[MAGPIE]** Kielmann, T., Hofman, R.F.H., Bal, H.E., Plaat, A., & Bhoedjang, R.A.F. (1999). *MagPIe: MPI's Collective Communication Operations for Clustered Wide Area Systems.* PPoPP 1999, pp. 131–140. → Hierarchical collective algorithms for wide-area clusters with heterogeneous tier bandwidths; introduces the principle of "do most of the work on the fastest tier" that informs the inner-vs-outer tier tradeoff in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §2. <https://doi.org/10.1145/301104.301116>

**[ZGGT09]** Zhu, H., Goodell, D., Gropp, W., & Thakur, R. (2009). *Hierarchical Collectives in MPICH2.* EuroPVM/MPI 2009, LNCS 5759. → An implementation of the MPI collectives on message-passing primitives that exploits the two-level hierarchy of multi-core clusters (within a node, between nodes). Backs up the tier-aware framing of `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §2. <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-03770-2_41>

**[HAN]** Luo, X., Wu, W., Bosilca, G., Pei, Y., Cao, Q., Patinyasakdikul, T., Zhong, D., & Dongarra, J. (2020). *HAN: a Hierarchical AutotuNed Collective Communication Framework.* IEEE CLUSTER 2020. → Open MPI's hierarchical collective framework: a collective is split by hardware level (intra-node, inter-node), each level running its own tuned algorithm. Backs up the "different primitive per tier" framing of `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §2. <https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9229573>

**[NCCL-PAT]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2024). *NCCL 2.23 Release Notes* (adds the PAT algorithm for AllGather and ReduceScatter). Accompanying blog: "New Scaling Algorithm and Initialization with NVIDIA Collective Communications Library 2.23," NVIDIA Developer Blog. Algorithm paper: Jeaugey, S. (2025). *PAT: a new algorithm for all-gather and reduce-scatter operations at scale.* arXiv:2506.20252. → PAT (Parallel Aggregated Trees): starts from the reversed-dimension Bruck schedule (far dimension first for all-gather); a logarithmic number of network transfers for small operations, with the payload aggregating per step; a logarithmic amount of internal buffering, independent of operation size; degrades toward a linear number of full-buffer transfers for large operations. Shipping scope in NCCL 2.23: inter-node AG / RS only, at 1 rank per node (the NCCL 2.31 release notes add hierarchical kernels — NVLS within the node, PAT across nodes — disabled by default). Backs up `01_collective_algorithms.md` §6 (scale-out rationale) and `01_collective_algorithms.md` Appendix A (full derivation). <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-23-4.html>, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20252>

**[DEMYST-NCCL]** Hu, Z., Shen, S., Bonato, T., Jeaugey, S., et al. (2025). *Demystifying NCCL: An In-depth Analysis of GPU Communication Protocols and Algorithms.* arXiv:2507.04786. → Source-level analysis of NCCL 2.19 — its protocols (Simple, LL, LL128), intra- and inter-node data transfer, and the Ring and Tree algorithms (NVLS and CollNet are outside its scope); the paper presents this as the foundation of the ATLAHS network simulator. Its benchmark, on 16 GH200 nodes of the Alps system (a 150 GB/s intra-node interconnect, Cray Slingshot between nodes), finds that "in both intra- and inter-node settings, the Ring algorithm excels for large messages, whereas the Tree algorithm performs best for smaller messages"; it gives no numeric crossover point. It also notes (§V-E) that the tree's "branching structure is built only across nodes. Inside each node, NCCL links the local GPUs in a simple chain." Backs up the "practice caveat" at the end of `01_collective_algorithms.md` §5.3 and the DBT-depth caveat in `02_topology_mapping.md` §2.3. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04786>

## Topology architecture papers

**[TPU-V4]** Jouppi, N.P., et al. (2023). *TPU v4: An Optically Reconfigurable Supercomputer for Machine Learning with Hardware Support for Embeddings.* ISCA 2023. → 3D torus with optical circuit switching for slice reconfiguration; the twisted torus improves all-to-all throughput over the regular torus by 1.63× on a 4 × 4 × 8 slice and by 1.31× on a 4 × 8 × 8 slice (§2.8, Figure 6) — a wiring effect on asymmetric slices. Backs up the TPU torus deployment note in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4 and the OCS slice-reshaping commentary in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.6; its slice-geometry facts (§2.5, Table 2) are used in `02_topology_mapping.md` §1.2, §4.2 and Appendix A; the assumed torus placeholder $\eta_\beta = 0.60 \approx 1/1.63$ in `05_contention_and_congestion.md` §4.1 borrows its size from the 1.63× ratio, which is not a contention measurement — the paper is not a calibration source for it, and the placeholder's $\eta_\alpha = 1.20$ has no public source. Table 2 (28% of the sampled slices twisted, the twisted 4 × 4 × 8 the most used shape at 16.0%) and Table 4 (inter-chip interconnect: 6 links at 50 GB/s on TPU v4, 4 links at 70 GB/s on TPU v3) are cited in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4 and §3.6. <https://doi.org/10.1145/3579371.3589350>

**[TRN2-ARCH]** Amazon Web Services. (2024–2025). *Amazon EC2 Trn2 Architecture.* AWS Neuron Documentation. → Trn2 server: 16 Trainium2 chips arranged as a 2D NeuronLink torus (each chip connects to 4 neighbors). Trn2 UltraServer: four Trn2 instances joined via a Z-dimension NeuronLink into a 3D 64-chip torus. Its specification table lists NeuronLink-v3 bandwidth of 1,024 GiB/s per chip inside an instance and 256 GiB/s per chip between the instances of an UltraServer (non-uniform link bandwidth, noted in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4). Backs up the Trainium topology notes in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4 and §3.6. <https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/about-neuron/arch/neuron-hardware/trn2-arch.html>

**[TPU-V5P]** Google Cloud. (2023–2026). *TPU v5p* (Cloud TPU documentation) and "Introducing Cloud TPU v5p and AI Hypercomputer" (Google Cloud Blog, December 2023). → 8,960 chips per pod in a 3D torus; 64-chip cubes joined by optical links and optical circuit switches, with chips "interconnected with reconfigurable high-speed links". Backs up the v5p pod size in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4 and the v5p slice-reconfiguration remark in §3.6. <https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/v5p>, <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-cloud-tpu-v5p-and-ai-hypercomputer>

**[KNL]** Sodani, A., et al. (2016). *Knights Landing: Second-Generation Intel Xeon Phi Product.* IEEE Micro 36(2):34–46; and Sodani, A. (2015), *Knights Landing (KNL): 2nd Generation Intel Xeon Phi Processor*, Hot Chips 27 slides. → 36 tiles connected by a 2D mesh interconnect, each tile's caching / home agent being its connection point to the mesh; rows and columns are half rings (no wraparound), YX routing. Backs up the on-die mesh example in `02_topology_mapping.md` §4.2 and §5.1. <https://doi.org/10.1109/MM.2016.25>, <https://doi.org/10.1109/HOTCHIPS.2015.7477467>

**[PCIE-MCAST]** Broadcom (PLX Technology). (2013). *PEX 8649 Data Book*, v1.5 (section 8.6 "Multicast", 8.6.7 "Multicast Extended Capability"); Microchip Technology, *Switchtec PFX / PFX-I Gen 3* and *Switchtec PFX-L Gen 3* family product briefs ("Supports multicast groups per port"). → PCIe switches that implement multicast. Backs up the PCIe multicast paragraph in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §1.2. <https://docs.broadcom.com/doc/PEX_8649-AA_Data_Book_v1.5_25Jan13.pdf>

**[NEURON-CC]** Amazon Web Services. (2024–2025). *Intra-node Collective Communications with AWS Neuron.* AWS Neuron Documentation. → Describes four intra-node algorithm families for AllGather, ReduceScatter and AllReduce on Trainium's 2D torus — Ring, Mesh, KangaRing and Recursive Doubling-Halving (RDH, introduced with "The 2D-Torus connectivity can also be seen as a 4D hyper-cube") — with an empirical "sweet range" for each: Mesh below 1 MB, RDH for 1–56 MB, KangaRing above 56 MB, Ring as "fallback only". Backs up the NeuronX CCL references in `02_topology_mapping.md` §3.4, §5.1 and Appendix A. <https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/neuron-runtime/explore/intranode-collective-comm.html>

**[DGX-SUPERPOD]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2023–2025). *NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD Reference Architecture* (the DGX H100 edition is linked; the GB200 and GB300 editions are [DGX-SUPERPOD-GB200] and [DGX-SUPERPOD-GB300]). → Reference architecture family for InfiniBand-based AI training clusters: a non-blocking ($s = 1$), rail-optimized fat-tree compute fabric built from Scalable Units (SUs). Backs up the production-Clos deployment claim in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1. <https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-h100/latest/>

**[DGX-SUPERPOD-GB200]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2024–2025). *NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD Reference Architecture Featuring NVIDIA DGX GB200 Systems* — Network Fabrics and Key Components pages. → Compute fabric of 64-port Quantum-2 QM9700 switches at 400 Gb/s (NDR): four ConnectX-7 per compute tray, 18 trays and 72 GPUs per rack, 4 rails; one SU = 8 racks = 576 GPUs with one spine-leaf group per rail (8 leaf + 6 spine each, one leaf per rack); rail-optimized, non-blocking fat-tree; a core tier is added from 2 SUs (4 SUs / 2304 GPUs: 128 leaf, 96 spine, 36 core). The SHARP statement is on the Key Components page, not the Network Fabrics page: "The fabric is designed to support the latest SHARPv3 features." Backs up the GB200-generation comparisons in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1.1, §1.2 and Appendix A.4, and the SHARPv3 note in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §2.2. <https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-gb200/latest/network-fabrics.html>, <https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-gb200/latest/dgx-superpod-components.html>

**[DGX-SUPERPOD-GB300]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2025). *NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD: Next Generation Scalable Infrastructure for AI Factories — Reference Architecture Featuring NVIDIA DGX GB300 Systems.* → Compute fabric of 144-port Quantum-X800 Q3400 switches at 800 Gb/s (XDR): four ConnectX-8 per compute tray; rail-optimized, non-blocking, full fat-tree; one SU = 8 racks = 576 GPUs with eight leaf switches, two per rail; a two-layer fabric up to 16 SUs (9,216 GPUs); Table 3 gives 32 leaf + 18 spine switches for 4 SUs (2304 GPUs); Figure 8 draws every spine linked to the leaves of all four rails. Backs up the rail-optimized layout of `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1.1, the outer-tier bandwidth of §1.2 and the switch counts of Appendix A. <https://docs.nvidia.com/pdf/dgx-spod-gb300-ra.pdf>

**[NCCL-PXN]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2022). *Doubling all2all Performance with NVIDIA Collective Communication Library 2.12.* NVIDIA Technical Blog. → PXN (PCI × NVLink): a GPU can send through a NIC that is not its own by first moving the data over NVLink to the GPU attached to that NIC; on rail-optimized fabrics (NIC $i$ of every node on leaf $i$) this keeps messages on one rail's leaf switch and reduces traffic through the spine tier. Backs up the NVLink rail bridge in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1.1 and §2.2. <https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/doubling-all2all-performance-with-nvidia-collective-communication-library-2-12/> Further sources for the PXN statements in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1.1 and §2.2: the NCCL user guide's environment-variable page (`NCCL_PXN_DISABLE`, `NCCL_P2P_PXN_LEVEL`, `NCCL_PXN_C2C`) <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/user-guide/docs/env.html>; the NCCL release notes 2.27.3 ("P2C (PXN over C2C)" added) <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-27-3.html>, 2.27.5 ("Fixed PXN support on MNNVL systems") <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-27-5.html> and 2.28.3 ("Enabled PXN over C2C by default") <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-28-3.html>; and NCCL source at tags v2.23.4-1, v2.27.3-1 and v2.28.3-1 — `src/graph/paths.cc` (the relay GPU must be "on the same node as us"; the `PXN_C2C` default), `src/graph/search.cc` (relay choice for send / receive), `src/transport/net.cc` (send side only; the sender writes into the relay's buffer) <https://github.com/NVIDIA/nccl>.

**[META-RSC]** Meta AI. (2022). *Introducing the AI Research SuperCluster — Meta's cutting-edge AI supercomputer for AI research.* Meta AI Blog, Jan 2022. → Meta Research SuperCluster (RSC): 760 DGX A100 systems (6,080 A100 GPUs) at launch, planned to grow to 16,000 GPUs, on what the post calls "an NVIDIA Quantum 1600 Gb/s InfiniBand two-level Clos fabric that has no oversubscription" (it gives no per-link speed); representative of production hyperscaler AI training deployments. Backs up the production-Clos deployment claim in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1. <https://ai.meta.com/blog/ai-rsc/>

## In-network collectives

**[SHARP-IB]** Graham, R.L., et al. (2016). *Scalable Hierarchical Aggregation Protocol (SHArP): A Hardware Architecture for Efficient Data Reduction.* COMHPC Workshop at SC16. → Original SHARP specification for InfiniBand; hardware-offloaded reduction trees, implemented in the SwitchIB-2 ASIC; reports small-message latency against host-based reduction on 128 hosts — a factor of 2.1 for an 8-byte `MPI_Allreduce` (6.01 → 2.83 μs), 3.24 at 4096 bytes with pipelining (46.93 → 14.48 μs), and almost four at 2048 bytes; it reports no bandwidth-utilization figure. Backs up the in-network reduction (switch ALU) mechanism in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §1.1 and the Quantum SHARP commercial discussion in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §2.2. <https://network.nvidia.com/pdf/solutions/hpc/paperieee_copyright.pdf>

**[NVLINK-SHARP]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2023–2025). *NVLink SHARP (NVLS).* NCCL 2.17.1 release notes (NVLink SHARP reduction / broadcast support for intra-node all-reduce); NCCL 2.18.1 release notes ("inter-node algorithms for NVLink SHARP: NVLink SHARP + IB SHARP (NVLS), NVLink SHARP + Tree (NVLSTREE)"); GB200 NVL Multi-Node Tuning Guide, NCCL chapter (NVLS is enabled by default where supported; `NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE`). → NVSwitch-based in-network reduction and multicast. Backs up the NVLS mechanism in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §1.1 and the scale-up INC summary in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §2.1. Neither source publishes busbw numbers; the measured lift quoted in this series comes from [NCCL-NVLS-REPORTS]. <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-17-1.html>, <https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/release-notes/rel_2-18-1.html>, <https://docs.nvidia.com/multi-node-nvlink-systems/multi-node-tuning-guide/nccl.html>

**[NCCL-NVLS-REPORTS]** Community reports in NVIDIA's public issue trackers (not vendor-published benchmarks). `NVIDIA/nccl-tests` issue #312 (May 2025): an 8-GPU all-reduce run with NVLS enabled reports busbw of 470–482 GB/s at 1–8 GB messages (and about 239 GB/s, 123 μs, at 16 MiB — the caveat in `05_contention_and_congestion.md` §5.1); the reporter does not name the GPU model, gives 450 GB/s as the theoretical figure for H20 / H100 / H200, and states (without measuring it in that run) a typical practical maximum of about 360 GB/s. `NVIDIA/nccl` issue #1221 (March 2024): on 8×H100 nodes, enabling NVLS moved bus bandwidth from about 330 to about 430 GB/s on one or two nodes (no gain beyond two). → The ~1.3× NVLS lift and the 360 / 450 ratio behind the crossbar $\eta_\beta \approx 0.80$ and the NVLS $\eta_\beta \approx 0.52$ in `05_contention_and_congestion.md` §4.1, and the lift quoted in `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §3 and `04_in_network_collectives.md` §1.4 and §4; indicative values only. <https://github.com/NVIDIA/nccl-tests/issues/312>, <https://github.com/NVIDIA/nccl/issues/1221>

**[NCCL-NVLS-2.27]** NVIDIA Corporation. (2025). *Enabling Fast Inference and Resilient Training with NCCL 2.27.* NVIDIA Technical Blog, Jul 2025. → NCCL 2.27 brings SHARP support to AllGather and ReduceScatter when NVLink SHARP is combined with IB SHARP; its symmetric-memory kernels give up to 2.5× on small-to-medium messages. ### Known numerical caveats (documented in-line)

- Switch cut-through latencies (100–200 ns for NVSwitch, 200–400 ns or a rounded 0.5 μs for InfiniBand) are estimates: no vendor document read for this series states them, except Tomahawk Ultra's 250 ns `[TH-ULTRA]`. "NVSwitch Gen3 / Gen4" are this series' shorthand for the H100-era and NVL72-era NVLink switches; NVIDIA's NCCL guide speaks of NVLS on "NVLink Version 3 switches and later" `[NVLINK-SHARP]`.
- The $\alpha$ values used in worked examples ($\alpha = 0.5\\,\mu$s intra-NVLink; $\alpha \approx 2\\,\mu$s / $8\\,\mu$s for the same-ToR / via-spine InfiniBand paths of `03_hierarchical_topologies.md` §1.2) are order-of-magnitude estimates; 0.5 μs corresponds to NVSwitch cut-through (~100 ns) plus endpoint software (~400 ns). Exact values depend on the NCCL path chosen at runtime. The model's conclusions are robust to $\pm 2\times$ variation on $\alpha$ at bulk payloads, where the bandwidth term dominates — above roughly 1 MB for INC AR and 4 MB for DBT AR at $N = 512$ (`04_in_network_collectives.md` §3.2); ring AR at $N = 512$ stays α-dominated up to about 230 MB.
- The $2\\,\alpha_{\mathrm{switch}}$ latency of every INC primitive is a hardware floor. NCCL's NVLS path had a fixed latency of about 24–25 μs in the measurements available (a May 2025 8-GPU user run in `[NCCL-NVLS-REPORTS]` issue #312, NCCL version not stated; the `hwLat` constant in NCCL v2.23.4 `src/graph/tuning.cc`); NCCL 2.27 reports up to 9× lower small-message latency with symmetric-memory kernels on NVLink `[NCCL-NVLS-2.27]`, with no NVLS-specific figure. The small-$M$ INC advantage tabulated in `04_in_network_collectives.md` §3.2 is therefore the mechanism's ceiling, not a measured result.
- The A2A on star entries in `02_topology_mapping.md` §2.5 and §5.1 use the per-rank pairwise direct-send formula (NVSwitch aggregate bisection saturated), appropriate for MoE workloads where all ranks participate simultaneously.
